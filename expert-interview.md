# Expert Interview: Murat

The interview has not yet taken place. The questions below are retained as a guide for later knowledge acquisition. For each important decision, we will ask for a concrete example, the conditions considered, the chosen action, and any exceptions. We will distinguish confirmed expert knowledge from our initial assumptions.

### Experience and priorities

1. What are your main responsibilities at People of Coffee, and which areas of café operations do you have the most practical expertise in?
2. What decisions do you personally make during a normal day? Which can staff make independently?
3. Which decisions are hardest for a new shift lead to make correctly?
4. When several things need attention, how do you decide what comes first?
5. What mistakes happen most often, and which have the biggest consequences?

### Menu and available quantities

6. What drinks, desserts, and other food do you offer? Which are made in-house, bought ready-made, or prepared from purchased ingredients?
7. How do you track available quantities, such as cakes, slices, portions, and ingredients?
8. For each item, what are the usual starting quantity, batch size, preparation time, and usable storage period?
9. Which items share ingredients, equipment, or preparation steps?
10. How does demand change by time of day, weekday, season, weather, or events? How do you recognise an unusually busy or quiet day?

### Preparing the next batch

11. For each item, what triggers the next batch: one portion remaining, selling out, a particular time, expected demand, or something else?
12. How do you choose the batch quantity? Can you make a smaller batch?
13. How does the decision change near closing time?
14. What changes when an item sells faster or slower than expected?
15. When several items need preparing, which do you start first?
16. What ingredients, staff skills, equipment, workspace, and storage must be available before preparation can begin?
17. When do you deliberately let an item sell out?
18. Can you describe a case of preparing too much or starting too late? What should have triggered a different decision?

### Stock and ordering

19. Who checks stock, how is it tracked, and do you distinguish ready-to-sell food, prepared components, and raw ingredients?
20. What triggers an order for each important ingredient or purchased item?
21. How do you choose the order quantity: target stock, expected sales, fixed quantities, or another method?
22. What delivery days, ordering deadlines, lead times, minimum quantities, and package sizes affect orders?
23. How do shelf life, storage capacity, existing orders, and expected waste affect purchases?
24. When stock runs out before delivery, when do you buy locally, substitute, or stop selling an item?
25. Which substitutions are acceptable, who approves them, and when must customers or menu information be updated?
26. Can you walk us through a recent order, including what you knew, what you ordered, and why?

### Staff and shift decisions

27. What tasks and stations exist, and what skills does each require?
28. How do you recognise that a station needs extra help? What do you observe or measure?
29. Whom do you reassign, and which tasks can they postpone?
30. How do you resolve competing needs such as waiting customers, a nearly sold-out dessert, a delivery, and cleaning?
31. How do decisions change when someone is absent or fewer experienced staff are available?

### Possible extension: barista training

32. How do you decide that a new barista is ready to work independently on a task?
33. What training stages or skills must they complete, and how are they assessed?
34. When can trainees practise during service, and when must an experienced person take over?
35. Would advice about task eligibility and supervision be useful during a shift?

### Possible extension: menu changes

36. What prompts you to add, change, or remove an item?
37. What requirements must a new item satisfy concerning demand, preparation, ingredients, equipment, staff skills, storage, and profitability?
38. Do you trial new items, and how do you judge success?
39. Is this decision frequent enough for system support to be useful?

### System usefulness and validation

40. Who should use the advisor, and which decisions should it support first?
41. What information can users realistically enter during a busy shift, and what is often missing?
42. Should it recommend one action, prioritise several, or ask follow-up questions?
43. When should it refer a decision to Murat?
44. Can you provide typical situations, difficult exceptions, and the expected recommendations?
45. Can we review our rules and prototype with you later to validate our interpretation?
